Services

Support scoped to the project you actually have

Six ways to get help — from a single assignment to a full final-year build. Each service below lists what it includes, who it tends to help, and how to request it.

College Assignments

What it includes
  • Worked examples and explanations for subject-wise assignment questions.
  • Lab records, observation notes and calculation checks.
  • Formatting to your department's template, including references and headings.
Who it may help

Students who understand the topic but want the reasoning checked, the structure tightened, or the formatting brought in line with what the department expects.

How to request support

Share the question sheet or brief, your deadline and the required format. You'll get a clear reply about what can be reviewed and what you will need to complete yourself.

Enquire about assignments

Mini Projects

What it includes
  • Scope definition for a build you can finish in two to six weeks.
  • Working code with setup instructions for Python, Java, C++, IoT or data tasks.
  • A walkthrough session so you can explain how it works.
Who it may help

Students building their first substantial project, or anyone unsure whether their idea fits the time available before the submission date.

How to request support

Send the project brief or a description of the idea, your preferred language and your deadline. A realistic scope is suggested before any work is agreed.

Enquire about mini projects

Web Development

What it includes
  • Responsive front-end builds with HTML, CSS, JavaScript or React.
  • Accessibility and basic performance checks before handover.
  • Deployment guidance and written notes for running the project yourself.
Who it may help

Students whose project needs a working website — portfolios, dashboards, information systems or event sites — and who need help with layout, structure or getting it online.

How to request support

Share wireframes or a reference site, the pages you need, the deadline and any backend requirements. Front-end scope and handover details are agreed in writing.

Enquire about web development

Software Projects

What it includes
  • Application structure, database schema and API design.
  • Version-controlled code with comments and tests where they add value.
  • Architecture diagrams, documentation and a final walkthrough.
Who it may help

Final-year and capstone teams building a full application who need structure, code review or hands-on development support within a fixed submission window.

How to request support

Send the requirement document, team size, preferred stack and deadline. Modules, deliverables and review points are agreed before development starts.

Enquire about software projects

Documentation & Presentations

What it includes
  • Report structure, chapter planning and referencing guidance.
  • Diagrams, screenshots and formatting matched to your college template.
  • Slide decks and speaker notes for reviews and Viva presentations.
Who it may help

Students with completed work who need help presenting it clearly, meeting a format requirement, or turning scattered notes into a readable report.

How to request support

Share your draft or outline, the department template and the review date. You'll get feedback on structure first, then support with the final document or deck.

Enquire about documentation

Project Guidance

What it includes
  • Topic shortlisting and feasibility checks against your timeline.
  • Scheduled check-ins, code reviews and debugging sessions.
  • Review and Viva preparation, including likely questions and how to answer them.
Who it may help

Students who want a second opinion, a plan for the next few weeks, or someone to unblock a specific problem without taking the project over.

How to request support

Explain where the project currently stands and what is blocking progress. A support format — single session or ongoing check-ins — is suggested from there.

Process

How a request moves forward

The same four steps apply to every service, so you always know what happens next.

01

Share the brief

Send the requirement, deadline, format and any existing work through the enquiry form.

02

Agree the scope

You get a clear reply on what support is possible, what it covers and when it will be ready.

03

Work in the open

Progress is shared as it happens, with questions answered along the way rather than at the end.

04

Review and hand over

Files, documentation and a walkthrough are handed across so you can present and maintain the work.

Clear about what this is

ProjectHub is guidance and development support. The service helps you plan, build, review, debug, document and present academic project work — it does not offer grades, guaranteed results, or work that misrepresents your contribution.

Before requesting support, check your college's rules on outside help. If a request would break them, it will be declined rather than worked around.

Not sure which service fits?

Describe the project in a few lines. You'll get a recommendation on the right kind of support — or an honest answer that it is not needed.
